Project: a light-up toy prototype

This prototype has three LEDs and a push button. Two red LEDs are always on. The blue LED fades in and out continuously, like it is "breathing." While the button is held down, the blue LED goes dark, and when the button is released it comes back at whatever brightness the fade has reached.

2. Total current draw and battery life

  • Each red LED: 10 mA. The blue LED: 6.3 mA.
  • All LEDs on at the same time (2 red + 1 blue): 10 + 10 + 6.3 = 26.3 mA. The button draws current only while it is held (16.7 mA through the pull-up), and holding it turns the blue LED off, so it never adds on top of that total.
  • Below the limits. Each pin carries at most 10 mA, under the 20 mA per-pin recommendation (40 mA absolute max) for the Uno's ATmega328P. The total of about 26 mA is well under the chip's 200 mA total limit.
  • Battery life with a 1200 mAh battery: 1200 mAh / 26.3 mA ≈ 46 hours for the LED circuit alone, assuming an ideal battery.
  • The Uno board itself also draws roughly 45 mA (approximate, it varies by board). Including it, 1200 / (26.3 + 45) ≈ 17 hours. Real batteries deliver somewhat less than their rated capacity.

3. Measured LED voltage

I measured the blue LED with a multimeter. Because the LED is being faded with PWM, the meter shows the average of the fast on/off pulses, so the reading rose and fell with the fade instead of staying put: it went from about 0 up to a maximum as the duty cycle went from 0% to 100%. The maximum reading, when the LED is fully on, is the voltage that matters here. The typical forward voltage for a blue LED is about 3.1 V. I measured 20-0V with the meter on the 20 V range
